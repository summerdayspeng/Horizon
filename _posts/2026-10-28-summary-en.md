A Chinese team claims it achieved a real-time optical fiber transmission rate of 3.2 Pb/s, enough to carry more than 20,000 4K movies in one second. The reported test used 24-core fiber and a commercial-compatible hardware architecture and validated space-division multiplexing plus S+C+L multi-band transmission, according to Tom&\#x27;s Hardware. The claimed rate is nearly 30% above the team&\#x27;s earlier 2.5 Pb/s result, but it has not been independently verified; the report notes that a 5.29 Pb/s laboratory record used advanced or experimental equipment and signal processing.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 27, 03:45

**「Why multicore and multiband matter」** Conventional long-haul fiber carries one spatial lane and amplifies mainly in the C band, so capacity gains must come from better coding and modulation within a fixed spectrum. Space-division multiplexing sidesteps that by adding parallel cores — this experiment used a 24-core fiber over a 10.3 km link — while S+C+L multiband transmission widens the usable spectrum beyond the conventional C band. Records also differ in how they are measured: offline laboratory results can lean on experimental signal processing, whereas a &quot;real-time&quot; figure requires the signal to be transmitted and recovered live, which is the distinction the team draws against the earlier 5.29 Pb/s laboratory mark.

**「Impact」** For network operators and equipment developers, the result highlights space-division multiplexing and S+C+L multi-band transmission as a route to higher real-time capacity, but the 3.2 Pb/s figure remains a team claim rather than demonstrated commercial throughput until it is independently replicated.

Tailscale has published an investigation and remediation update concerning vulnerabilities in Tailscale SSH. The supplied material does not specify affected versions, CVE identifiers, exploitation reports, or a remediation timeline, so those details should be confirmed against Tailscale&\#x27;s advisory and current releases.

hackernews · figmert · Oct 27, 19:55 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50264857)

**「Background: Tailscale SSH」** Tailscale SSH lets approved devices log in to one another over a private Tailscale network, with access governed by Tailscale&\#x27;s access-control system instead of separately managed SSH keys. Before the remediation update, security coverage reported that almost a dozen flaws in Tailscale SSH could be exploited to steal credentials, crash servers, or gain root privileges, and that Tailscale was working to patch them. Tailscale&\#x27;s ACLs are its original network-layer access mechanism; the company now recommends grants, though it says ACLs will remain supported.

**「Patch and ACL review」** Teams that grant SSH through Tailscale SSH ACLs should treat the grants as potentially broader than intended: TS-2026-009 describes insecure argument handling that permitted root access, so an ACL meant to allow only SSH into a node may not have contained the blast radius. Remediation requires updating tailscaled, with a patch reported as expected within a day; until it is deployed, narrowing or disabling Tailscale SSH ACL grants on sensitive nodes is the practical containment.

**「Community discussion」** A former Tailscale employee now managing Tailscale at an organization said their review of the vulnerabilities and the fixes landing on GitHub indicates impact is limited to nodes where ACLs permit Tailscale SSH access but do not provide a path to sudoers or root, and that users without SSH access or connectivity to a vulnerable node cannot exploit them. Other commenters argued against letting Tailscale handle both VPN connectivity and SSH authentication, calling it an added single point of failure and attack surface, with some recommending disabling Tailscale SSH in favor of stock OpenSSH and separately distributed keys.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 28, 01:46

**「Why &\#x27;sleep&\#x27; for language models」** Continual learning — updating a model on new data without retraining from scratch — runs into the stability–plasticity dilemma: online updates can cause catastrophic forgetting and geometric drift, while privacy-preserving methods that avoid data replay tend to sacrifice knowledge transfer. Borrowing from neuroscience, earlier work has explored giving language models a &quot;sleep&quot; phase in which the model stops processing new data and instead uses self-generated data to consolidate recent memories offline, alongside a multi-frequency memory hierarchy. Google Research&\#x27;s proposal extends that offline-consolidation idea into a repeating cycle that alternates active interaction with consolidation phases, which is the mechanism the reported benchmark comparisons are meant to test.

**「Operational impact」** Adopting the framework is not an inference-only change: developers must add a training loop that alternates active interaction with offline consolidation phases. That means teams need to schedule and budget extra compute for the consolidation runs rather than treating continual learning as a purely streaming update.
