### [Quad9 to Exit France After DNS Blocking Dispute](https://quad9.net/news/blog/au-revoir-france/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Quad9 plans to exit France, and its CTO says the public DNS resolver will probably use geoIP filters to deny French users access after November 24. The move follows a French court dispute over DNS blocking and geoIP circumvention that resulted in a contempt fine; a quoted passage in the discussion says Sony Music Entertainment GmbH used a VPN from Germany to reach content blocked by geoIP, and the court found Quad9 noncompliant. The CTO said the focus on how to block French users misses Quad9&\#x27;s argument that geoIP blocking is insufficient for selectively denying resolution of certain names.

hackernews · farrokhi · Oct 22, 14:32 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50195959)

**「Background」** Quad9 is a Swiss nonprofit public DNS resolver. Before the exit announcement, a French court ordered it to block 58 domains or face a €580,000 fine, and the dispute escalated when Sony Music used a VPN from Germany to access blocked content in circumvention of geoIP blocking, which the court treated as noncompliance, according to a Hacker News comment from someone identifying as Quad9&\#x27;s CTO.

**「Impact」** French users and organizations relying on Quad9 should plan to switch to another DNS resolver before the stated November 24 cutoff, since geoIP filtering would make Quad9&\#x27;s service unavailable to them.

**「Discussion」** Commenters largely criticized the outcome as misplaced punishment for an intermediary, with one comparing it to holding a phone book liable for listing a criminal&\#x27;s number and another arguing DNS resolvers should stop resolving domains owned by companies that sue them. A further commenter said Cisco made a similar exit about two years earlier after Canal+ obtained web-blocking injunctions against Google, Cloudflare and Cisco.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters focused less on the exploit itself than on its implications and tooling: hturan speculated that read/write decrypted filesystem access could quickly lead to game dumps playable on arbitrary hardware, while valgaze described an automated setup using JetKVM, a SwitchBot-powered button presser, and Codex. rootsudo questioned whether Codex&\#x27;s restrictions make it usable for security research, and KeplerBoy argued the automation tooling is notable regardless of the AI&\#x27;s role.

### [eMudhra to exit global SSL/TLS certificate business](https://emudhra.com/en/press-release/emudhra-to-exit-global-ssl-tls-certificate-business) ⭐️ 8.0/10

eMudhra announced in a press release that it will exit the global SSL/TLS certificate business, a decision the company ties to what it calls social-media scrutiny &quot;out of proportion to SSL/TLS&\#x27;s place in our business.&quot; The release, as quoted by commenters, says eMudhra&\#x27;s global SSL/TLS infrastructure runs separately from its other operations and that &quot;there were no end entity certificates issued out of the affected CA&\#x27;s&quot; — a claim commenters disputed, since anyone can request certificates from a CA. The supplied material gives no exit timeline, no explanation of the underlying incident, and no root-cause account beyond the company&\#x27;s framing of the response as disproportionate.

hackernews · bo0tzz · Oct 22, 13:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50195110)

**「Background」** eMudhra is an Indian certificate authority, and its exit decision came five days after a researcher reported recovering the private keys for eight of its intermediate CA certificates. The Register reported that security researcher Scott Helme found one of the exposed private keys was &quot;the number 4,&quot; a trivially guessable value. Because intermediate CAs sign end-entity certificates, compromise of their private keys can undermine trust in every certificate chained to them.

**「Impact」** Organizations holding publicly trusted TLS certificates from eMudhra&\#x27;s global CA will need to plan a migration to another certificate authority, and the absence of a stated schedule in the supplied material leaves the replacement window unclear. Commenters also pointed to the Chrome team&\#x27;s public question set filed in Mozilla&\#x27;s bug tracker \(bug 2083449\) as evidence that browser root-program scrutiny, not just social media, drove the decision — the audience that would normally adjudicate eMudhra&\#x27;s trust status.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters were largely unconvinced by the announcement: rdtsc argued that without an apology or an explanation of whether the failure was human or procedural, the exit &quot;looks pretty bad on them,&quot; while luispa posted a one-character RSA private key as a joke about key strength. Others, including Toutouxc, said the episode defied analogy, and ieie3366 pointed readers to the Mozilla bug thread containing the Chrome team&\#x27;s questions to eMudhra.

### [NVIDIA&\#x27;s OpenShell proposes a sandboxed runtime for autonomous AI agents](https://github.com/NVIDIA/OpenShell) ⭐️ 7.0/10

NVIDIA has published OpenShell on GitHub, presented as a safe, private runtime for fleets of autonomous AI agents. The pitch quoted in discussion is that agents are most useful when they can read files, install packages, call APIs and use credentials, and OpenShell is meant to grant that access without unrestricted reach into data, secrets or the network. The supplied material contains no README details, version number, benchmarks or adoption evidence, so the isolation guarantees and network controls cannot be independently assessed.

hackernews · niemandhier · Oct 22, 17:44 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50198675)

**「Background」** Agents are normally launched with the permissions of the user or service account running them, so a runtime layer that mediates filesystem, network, and credential access sits between the agent and the host machine. NVIDIA&\#x27;s developer documentation describes OpenShell as supplying sandboxed execution environments intended to protect data, credentials, and infrastructure, and the product page frames it as governing how an agent executes, what it can see and do, and where inference goes.

**「What changes for agent operators」** For teams running agent fleets, access control moves from prompt-level instructions to a kernel-enforced, per-operation policy: every file access, system call, and network connection is checked at runtime, so an agent can be given credentials and package-install rights without inheriting the host user&\#x27;s full reach. That makes policy authoring — and OpenShell&\#x27;s formal check of what a policy change would allow before it is applied — part of the deployment workflow rather than an afterthought, particularly for organizations adopting NVIDIA&\#x27;s newly announced Reference Architecture for enterprise and edge infrastructure.

**「Community discussion」** One commenter recommends iron proxy for domain-based network restrictions and credential swapping, but says preventing exfiltration of the codebases an agent works on remains unsolved. Others describe OpenShell as a useful way to manage agent containers, while one commenter argues that the &quot;all the access, none of the risk&quot; framing can only strike a balance rather than guarantee safety.

### [Triangulation writeup claims faster-than-earcut map polygon performance](https://globus.software/blog/2026/10/16/cutting-map-polygons/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A performance-focused writeup reports triangulating 3.7 million map polygons in 489 ms and claims faster results than the earcut library. The 489 ms figure is the author&\#x27;s benchmark rather than an independent measurement. For developers working on map rendering or computational geometry, the discussion highlights tradeoffs beyond raw speed, including bundle size and algorithm choice.

hackernews · molind · Oct 22, 09:31 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50193170)

**「Background」** Polygon triangulation splits a shape into triangles so a graphics pipeline can render it. Earcut is a JavaScript library for that task, billed as the fastest and smallest option for WebGL apps at about 4KB gzipped and based on ear-clipping and FIST ideas \(tool-2-2, tool-2-3\). That makes Earcut the baseline for the post&\#x27;s claim of triangulating 3.7 million map polygons in 489 ms.
