### [Report: eMudhra CA keys had 8 bits of entropy, cracked in milliseconds](https://scotthelme.co.uk/eight-bits-of-trust-emudhras-ca-keys-cracked-in-milliseconds/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A report published on scotthelme.co.uk alleges that certificate authority eMudhra generated intermediate CA keys with only eight bits of entropy, leaving 256 possible keys that could be recovered by brute force in milliseconds. A recovered intermediate key would let an attacker mint certificates that appear valid to anything trusting that chain, exposing relying parties until the intermediates are distrusted. A summary relayed in the comments claims eight intermediates were affected \(six able to sign TLS certificates, two for code signing\) and that Mozilla and Google moved to block them after an October 17 report, but the supplied item contains only the report&\#x27;s title and reader discussion, so those counts and vendor timelines are unconfirmed here.

hackernews · hkc · Oct 21, 09:08 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50177916)

**「Why intermediate CA keys matter」** Intermediate CA certificates are delegated signing authorities chained to a root that browsers and operating systems already trust, so recovery of an intermediate&\#x27;s private key lets an attacker issue certificates that appear to chain back to that trusted root. Mozilla&\#x27;s incident timeline dates the eMudhra key compromise to October 17, 2026, when researcher Wayne reported it, with proof of private-key control, to browser root programs and eMudhra.

**「What operators must do」** Anyone shipping a CA bundle — OS ca-certificates packages, certifi, Node, Homebrew, and the container base images built on them — needs to rebuild once those bundles drop the affected eMudhra intermediates, since the recovered keys let anyone mint certificates that verify against them until the distrust is distributed. The unresolved exposure is devices whose trust stores cannot be updated; commenters noted these remain vulnerable, and one pointed to reports that the weak keys are already being used to forge certificates for such hardware.

**「Community discussion」** Commenter john\_strinlai relayed a note from the original reporter saying the weak keys were found with Google&\#x27;s paranoid\_crypto tool &quot;out of the box&quot; and that a fuller scan of all CCADB certificates turned up no further hits. Other commenters argued the difficult part is hardware that cannot be updated, with one asking whether the recovered keys could be used to jailbreak such devices.
